Does Your Shop Really Need a Website in 2026?

October 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: yes — but probably not the website you're picturing. You don't need a blog. You don't need animations. You need a digital version of your shop sign, your business card, and your front counter, open 24 hours.

What customers actually do

When someone's water heater dies at 9pm, they Google "plumber near me." They skim three results and call the one that looks legitimate. "Legitimate" means: a real website with the services listed, a phone number they can tap, and reviews. No website — or a broken Facebook page from 2019 — and you're invisible at the exact moment someone wants to pay you.

What a shop site needs (the short list)

  • What you do — services, plainly listed. No jargon.
  • How to reach you — tap-to-call phone number, big and obvious, plus a simple quote form for after hours.
  • Where you are — service area and a map. "Serving the greater Springfield area" beats a street address for most trades.
  • Proof — a few real reviews or photos of real work. Phone photos are fine.
  • Hours — including whether you do emergency calls.

That's it. Five things. Anything beyond that is decoration.

What it doesn't need

  • A blog you'll never update (a dead blog looks worse than no blog).
  • Stock photos of smiling models in hard hats. Customers can tell.
  • Fancy animations that slow down phones — where most of your customers are.

"But I get all my work from referrals"

Great — and what does the referral do before calling? They Google your name. A clean one-page site turns "my neighbor mentioned you" into a call. No site turns it into "hmm, let me check the other guy too."

The cheapest credible option
