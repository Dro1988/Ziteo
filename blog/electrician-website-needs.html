What an Electrician's Website Needs That a Business Card Site Doesn't

November 2026 · 5 min read

A business card site says a name and a phone number. That works for someone who already has your card — they already know who you are and what you do. It does almost nothing for the homeowner whose panel just died, because that homeowner does not search by your name. They search by the job: “panel upgrade near me,” “electrician for flickering lights,” “generator install.”

If your website only answers “who is this company?” it misses every search that starts with “who can fix this?” Here is what an electrician's site needs beyond the card.

1. A Page Per Job Type

Each service you offer is its own search, so each one needs its own page:

  • Panel upgrades — the homeowner with a full box, an old panel, or a renovation that needs more capacity
  • Troubleshooting — flickering lights, dead outlets, a breaker that keeps tripping; the urgent, open-ended searches
  • Generators — a considered purchase people research before storm season, not during an outage
  • Lighting — recessed lighting, fixtures, and the jobs homeowners plan rather than panic about
  • EV chargers — a fast-growing search from someone who just bought the car and needs the charger this week

One generic “Services” page with a bullet list buries all five of those searches on a page that matches none of them well. Five clear pages, each written for the one job, is how a stranger's search lands on your site instead of a directory's.

2. Call-First on Mobile

Most visitors arrive on a phone, and a lot of them arrive mid-problem — standing in a dark kitchen, looking at a dead panel. Build for that person:

  • Phone number tappable at the top of every page — one tap to call, no copying, no digging through a contact page
  • Short pages that load fast on a weak connection
  • The service and the town in the first screen — the visitor confirms in seconds that they found the right trade in the right place

A beautiful desktop site that hides the number on mobile is a business card with extra steps.

3. Service-Area Pages by Town

“Serving the metro area” is not a place anyone searches. People search their town. Name the towns you actually serve, on dedicated service-area pages, so a search for an electrician in that town has a page to land on that says the town's name back.

Keep the list honest: only towns you will actually drive to. A service-area page for a town you won't take work in wastes the customer's call and your drive time in equal measure.

4. Trust Signals Without Inventing Them

Homeowners letting someone rewire a panel want proof. The honest version is already in your shop — put it on the site:

  • Your license line, stated plainly where a visitor can find it
  • Your actual service list — the jobs above, no more, no less
  • Your coverage, by town, matching the service-area pages

What does not belong: testimonials you don't have, review badges nobody awarded, or a customer count you can't back up. A homeowner checking an electrician is checking for exactly that kind of inflation. The license line you really hold is worth more than five stars you don't.
