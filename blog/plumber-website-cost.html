How Much Does a Plumber Website Cost in 2026?

October 2026 · 5 min read

If you run a plumbing company, your website has one job: turn a homeowner with water on the floor into a phone call. Everything else is decoration. So what should that cost?

The honest answer is that it depends on who builds it and what you get for the money. Here are the four routes most plumbing shops take, with what they really cost.

Option 1: DIY Website Builder — Cheapest Cash, Most Expensive Time

Platforms like Wix or Squarespace run roughly $20–$40 a month, plus about $15–$20 a year for your domain name.

That sounds cheap until you count your hours. Building the site, writing the pages, figuring out why the contact form isn't sending — that's evenings you're not billing. And a builder site only works if a customer already knows your name. It does very little to help a stranger in your town find you when they search “plumber near me.”

Best for: brand-new shops that need something — anything — online this week.

Option 2: A Freelancer — $500 to $2,500, Results Vary

A local freelancer will usually charge somewhere between $500 and $2,500 upfront, sometimes with a small monthly fee for hosting and updates, sometimes with nothing — meaning when something breaks or you need a phone number changed, you're hunting them down.

Ask three questions before you pay anyone:

  1. Who owns the site and the domain when it's done?
  2. Who makes changes after launch, and what do changes cost?
  3. Is the site built to show up in Google for your town, or just to look nice?

A freelancer who can't answer the first question clearly is a red flag. Shops lose their websites every year because the domain was registered in someone else's name.

Option 3: An Agency — $3,000 and Up

Agencies charge agency prices: commonly $3,000–$10,000 to build, plus $100+/month retainers. You get a team and a polished result, but most plumbing shops are paying for process they don't need. If you're a one-to-five-truck operation, the math rarely works.

What a Plumber Website Actually Needs

Whatever route you pick, don't pay for features that don't get calls. A plumbing site needs:

  • Your phone number at the top of every page, tappable on a phone
  • A services page that names what you actually do — water heaters, drain cleaning, re-pipes — because that's what people search
  • Your service area named by town, not just “serving the metro area”
  • A contact form that works — test it yourself before launch, and again a month later
  • Fast load on a phone. Most emergency searches happen on mobile, often on bad connections

Things you do not need on day one: a blog with forty posts, a chat widget, animations, or a page about your “brand story.” Get found, get called, get paid. The rest can come later.

The Cost Nobody Quotes: Doing Nothing

A shop with no real website — or a dead one with an old phone number — doesn't look closed on Google. It looks worse than closed. It looks careless. Homeowners with a burst pipe call the first credible shop they find. If that's not you, the job went to whoever it was, and you'll never know it happened.

Bottom Line

  • DIY: ~$20–$40/month plus your time
  • Freelancer: ~$500–$2,500 upfront, support varies
  • Agency: ~$3,000+ upfront plus retainers
  • Ziteo done-for-you: $199 setup + $59/month, 30-day money-back on setup

Pick the route that matches your time and budget — but pick one. In 2026, “we get all our work from word of mouth” and “nobody can find us online” are the same sentence said two ways.
