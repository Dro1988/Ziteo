Do Contractors Need a Google Business Profile If They Already Have a Website?

November 2026 · 6 min read

Yes — because they are not the same thing, and one does not replace the other.

A website is your storefront page. A Google Business Profile is how the map finds you. A homeowner searching on their phone usually meets one of them first and checks the other before they call. If only one exists, half that check comes up empty.

Here is what each surface actually does, in plain terms.

1. What Each Surface Does

Your website:

  • Lists your services, job by job — the pages a search can land on
  • Carries your pricing honesty, your service area by town, and your hours
  • Gives a ready caller a tappable phone number and a contact path that you control

Your Google Business Profile / map listing:

  • Shows up for “near me” searches — “plumber near me,” “electrician near me,” “roofer in [town]”
  • Puts the call button and the directions button in front of someone who is ready to move, not browsing
  • Shows your name, phone, hours, and service area in the map result itself, before anyone clicks through

A profile with no website sends a ready caller to a thin listing with nowhere to learn more. A website with no profile can be exactly right for the job and still miss the map search entirely, because the map is a separate surface with its own listing.

2. Why Both, in Plain Terms

They point at each other, and each one makes the other easier to trust.

  • The profile gets you found on the map. The website answers the next question: “Do they do this job, in my town, and how do I call them?”
  • The facts should match on both — same business name, same phone, same towns served, same hours. A mismatch gives a caller a reason to move to the next name on the list.
  • Neither one is a substitute for the other. Paying for a good website does not create a map listing, and claiming a map listing does not give you service pages a search can rank.

For a contractor, that matters more than for most businesses. The call is usually local, usually on a phone, and often tied to one specific job in one specific town. That is exactly the search the two surfaces split between them.

3. The Honest Requirement, Stated Plainly

A Google Business Profile needs a verifiable business address and phone number before Google will verify and claim it. That is not a Ziteo rule — it is Google's verification step, and there is no honest way around it.

Stated just as plainly about Ziteo itself: Ziteo's own directory listings — Google, Yelp, Bing, and Apple — are still awaiting a business address and phone on the record. No listing is claimed in this post, and no listing should be described as claimed until that verification has actually happened. This post is advice for contractors about their own profiles, not an announcement that any Ziteo listing exists.

If you do not yet have an address and phone you can put through verification, that is not a dead end. It just changes what you do first — see the next section.

4. What to Do This Week With No Profile Yet

Make your website carry the same facts a profile would, so the site does the whole job until verification is possible — and so the two match on day one when it is:

  • Services, named plainly — one page per job type you actually do, not one generic “Services” list
  • Towns served, named by town — only towns you will actually drive to
  • Hours, stated as they are — including what happens outside them, if anything
  • A tappable phone number at the top of every page — one tap to call on mobile, no copying, no hunting through a contact page
  • Your license line, where it belongs — stated plainly, with no testimonials, review badges, or customer counts you cannot back up

That work is not wasted if you claim a profile later. It is the same work — the profile will need exactly those facts, and a website that already states them cleanly is ready to be linked from it.
