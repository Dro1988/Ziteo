How Long Does It Take to Get a Professional Website for Your Shop?

October 2026 · 5 min read

Most shop owners put off getting a website because they assume it's a months-long ordeal — endless meetings, vague invoices, and a launch date that keeps sliding. It doesn't have to be that way. Here's what a straightforward website build actually looks like, step by step, so you know exactly what you're signing up for.

Step 1: The First Conversation (Day 1)

This is the part people overthink. You don't need a marketing degree or a brand book. A good first call covers the basics: what your shop does, who your customers are, what services you want front and center, and how people should reach you — phone, text, contact form, or all three.

If you've got photos of your shop, your trucks, or your crew, even better. If you don't, say so. A real photo of your actual shop beats a stock photo of a smiling stranger every time, but the site can launch without them and add them later.

Time from you: about 30 minutes.

Step 2: Content and Structure (Days 2–5)

This is where most projects stall — not because the work is hard, but because the shop owner gets a homework assignment ("just write your services page!") and life gets in the way. You're running a business. You don't have time to become a copywriter.

The better approach: the person building your site writes the first draft of everything — services, about page, contact info — based on that first conversation. You review it, mark what to change, and it's done. One round of revisions is usually all it takes.

Time from you: one review, maybe an hour.

Step 3: Design and Build (Days 5–10)

With content approved, the site gets designed and built. For a local shop, this means a clean homepage, a services page, an about page, a contact page, and your phone number visible everywhere. Mobile-friendly isn't a bonus feature anymore — most of your customers will find you on their phones — so the site gets built mobile-first and tested on real phones before it goes live.

Nothing exotic. No animations that take ten seconds to load. A shop website has one job: make it dead simple for a customer to figure out what you do and call you.

Step 4: Launch (Around Day 10–14)

The site goes live on your domain, gets submitted to Google, and shows up correctly when people search your shop name and town. You get a quick walkthrough of how to request updates — because a website isn't a one-time product, it's something that stays current as your business changes.

So the honest answer: a professional shop website should take about two weeks from first call to launch, not two months. If someone quotes you a three-month timeline for a five-page site, they're either juggling too many clients or padding the invoice.

The Real Bottleneck Is Almost Never the Builder

In our experience, the thing that slows a website project down is almost never the technical work. It's the back-and-forth: waiting on a logo file, waiting on someone to approve the services list, waiting on photos that were "coming next week" three weeks ago.

The fastest sites we've seen launch are the ones where the owner gives us what they have on day one — even if it's imperfect — and lets us fill in the gaps. You can always swap in better photos later. A good site live today beats a perfect site that's still "in progress" six months from now.

Bottom Line

Two weeks. One 30-minute call, one round of revisions, and a site that brings in calls while you work. If your shop still doesn't have a website — or has one from 2014 that looks it — the barrier isn't time or money. It's just getting started.
