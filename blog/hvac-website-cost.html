How Much Does an HVAC Website Cost in 2026?

October 2026 · 6 min read

An HVAC company's website gets tested twice a year: the first hot week and the first cold snap. That's when a homeowner with no air or no heat searches, taps the first credible result, and calls. If your site is slow, outdated, or not there at all, that call goes somewhere else. So what should the site that catches that call cost?

It depends on who builds it and what you actually get. Here are the four routes most HVAC shops take, with what they really cost. The market ranges below are general market ranges, labeled as such — Ziteo's own price is stated exactly at the end so you can compare it against anything.

Option 1: DIY Website Builder — Cheapest Cash, Most Expensive Time

Platforms like Wix or Squarespace run roughly $20–$40 a month, plus about $15–$20 a year for your domain name.

The cash is cheap. Your hours are not. Building the pages, writing the service descriptions, and figuring out why the contact form isn't sending takes evenings you're not billing — and it tends to get postponed until it is suddenly peak season and there is no time at all. A builder site also only helps a customer who already knows your name. It does very little to help a stranger in your town find you when they search “AC repair near me” or “furnace repair near me.”

Best for: a brand-new shop that needs something — anything — online this week, and plans to replace it later.

Option 2: A Freelancer — $500 to $2,500, Results Vary

A local freelancer will usually charge somewhere between $500 and $2,500 upfront, sometimes with a small monthly fee for hosting and updates, sometimes with nothing — meaning when a phone number changes or a page breaks, you're hunting them down.

Ask three questions before you pay anyone:

  1. Who owns the site and the domain when it's done?
  2. Who makes changes after launch, and what do changes cost?
  3. Is the site built to show up in Google for your town and your services, or just to look nice?

A freelancer who can't answer the first question clearly is a red flag. Shops lose their websites every year because the domain was registered in someone else's name.

Option 3: An Agency — $3,000 and Up

Agencies charge agency prices: commonly $3,000–$10,000 to build, plus $100+/month retainers. You get a team and a polished result, but most HVAC shops are paying for process they don't need. If you're a one-to-five-truck operation, the math rarely works.

What an HVAC Website Actually Needs

Whatever route you pick, don't pay for features that don't get calls. An HVAC site needs:

  • Your phone number at the top of every page, tappable on a phone — no-heat and no-cool calls are urgent, and urgent callers do not hunt for a number
  • A separate page for each service you actually offer — AC repair, furnace repair, heat pumps, system installation, maintenance — because that is what people search, service by service
  • Your service area named by town, not just “serving the metro area”
  • Seasonal readiness — the site has to be fast and working before the first hot week and the first cold snap, not updated during them
  • A contact form that works — test it yourself before launch, and again a month later
  • Fast load on a phone. Most emergency searches happen on mobile, often on a weak connection in a house that is getting hotter by the hour

Things you do not need on day one: a blog with forty posts, a chat widget, animations, or a page about your “brand story.” Get found, get called, get the truck rolling. The rest can come later.

The Cost Nobody Quotes: Doing Nothing

A shop with no real website — or a dead one with an old phone number — doesn't look closed on Google. It looks worse than closed. It looks careless. Homeowners with no heat call the first credible shop they find. If that's not you, the job went to whoever it was, and you'll never know it happened. Peak season does not send you a warning before it tests your website.

Bottom Line

  • Freelancer: ~$500–$2,500 upfront, support varies
